NeuroBridge

AI today runs on hardware that wastes most of its energy. Brain-inspired chips that are 100× more efficient exist — but nobody can use them. There's no bridge between the two worlds. NeuroBridge is that bridge.

Why we're building it

Think of AI today like a house where every light is on, 24 hours a day. Running a single AI query on a GPU uses about as much electricity as leaving a light bulb on for hours. For companies like Google or Microsoft, that adds up to billions of dollars in electricity costs every year.

Researchers have built chips inspired by how the human brain works. Your brain recognises a face in milliseconds using less power than a dim light bulb. These "neuromorphic" chips work the same way — they only compute when something actually happens. The result: 25–100× better energy efficiency.

The problem? There's no standard way to connect them to the AI frameworks every developer already uses — PyTorch, JAX, ONNX. NeuroBridge is that cable.

The Problem
GPU-based AI inference is 100× more energy-intensive than biological neural computation. Datacenter energy costs are scaling out of control.
The Gap
Brain-inspired chips like Intel Loihi 2 and IBM NorthPole are dramatically more efficient — but there is no software layer connecting them to mainstream AI.
The Solution
NeuroBridge is a translation + execution + benchmarking layer. Bring your existing model. We convert it. Run on brain-inspired hardware — no rewrites.
What it looks like

# Load any existing PyTorch model — no changes needed model = torch.load("my_model.pt")   # One-line conversion to neuromorphic snn = Bridge(model, target="loihi2").convert()   # Benchmark against GPU baseline results = Benchmark(model, snn).run() print(results.summary())   Energy reduction: 47× Latency reduction: 62% Accuracy delta: -0.3%
Build phases
Phase 1

PyTorch model loader, SNN conversion, benchmark framework. Runs on CPU/GPU — no special hardware needed.
Requires: Python, PyTorch, Brian2 / Nengo

CellClock

A home lab experiment exploring whether synthetic genetic circuits can influence how long cells live. Using CRISPR-modified yeast, time-lapse microscopy, and AI-driven image tracking to observe aging patterns.

The question
Can we reprogram how long a cell lives by engineering the genetic circuits that control its internal clock?
The method
GFP-tagged yeast cells + synthetic oscillator + automated microscopy + OpenCV tracking over time.
Current status
ODIN kit ordered. GFP-yeast strain selected. USB microscope calibration underway. Tracking pipeline in design.
Longer vision
A closed-loop system where AI observes, analyzes, and eventually guides biological experiments autonomously.
