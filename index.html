Why Reverie exists

Two things I'm certain of: the brain is the most important system we still don't understand — and AI is the first tool powerful enough to help us finally understand it.

Reverie is where I think out loud about the place those two meet. Some of it is research. Some of it is a product I'm building. All of it points at the same future.

01 · Flagship Product

Lumira. →
An AI film director in your camera

For a century, making something cinematic was gated — by equipment, crews, and a language that took years to learn. Lumira removes the gate. You talk to a director who understands story, light, and shot, and it directs you in real time, then grades the footage like a colorist.

It exists for one reason: to make filmmaking accessible to everyone. The camera was never the missing piece — the director was. A billion people already tell stories on their phones. Lumira hands them the craft to make those stories real.

The Lab. →
Intelligence · Compute · Biology

Where the conviction turns into experiments. Two projects on the layer beneath — brain-inspired computation, and the biology of how living systems age.

NeuroBridge — an idea I want to build — asks what AI would look like if it ran the way the brain does, on a fraction of the energy. CellClock — an experiment I want to start — asks whether the clock inside a living cell can be rewritten. Different scales of one question: how intelligence and life actually compute.
